There is no counterexample to the stated narrow claims. The word “physical” in these names refers to the modeled row indices and decoded `Trace 9`; it does not itself establish a correspondence to an accepted native proof. In particular, `allArithmeticRootsZero` quantifies over `bytecodeArithmetic (fromTrace trace i)` on every row. That is the nine local arithmetic roots; it does not assert all Gate lookup constraints, public pin events, or native verifier acceptance. The synthetic trace fills rows outside the projected source cells with zero-add rows, so its fixed columns are not the native complete roster.

## Remaining soundness obligations

1. Prove or explicitly assume that production Python/Zig admission and emitted normalized bytes agree with Lean `SSATextBytes.parseBytes`, `SSANormalizedBytes.encodeNormalized`, and `checkRelation` for the admitted direct-Gate profile. These theorems cover that bounded profile, not the full S31 syntax or every chip.
2. Derive the complete native 512-row fixed roster and honest main trace from accepted source bytes: public input pack, copy, output mask/inverse, constant derivation, padding, and source rows. The four fixtures and the modeled `rowRole` partition do not discharge this universal compiler refinement.
3. Establish public input/output pin events, unique producer/use events, and exact Gate balance for an honest full trace. The source-slice existence theorem currently proves neither the ideal lookup relation nor non-vacuity of the earlier full `UnpartitionedTrace` assumptions.
4. Connect native verifier acceptance and authenticated PCS openings to that full decoded trace and its actual AIR roots and fixed columns. State Fiat–Shamir, commitment binding, sampling/FRI, field encoding, and lookup-compression assumptions at that bridge. No theorem reviewed here turns a native proof's Boolean verdict into its premises.

The newly added source-address canonicality and synthetic witness close meaningful local gaps. They should be cited as **source-slice correspondence**, not as the full compiler-correspondence or native proof soundness theorem.
